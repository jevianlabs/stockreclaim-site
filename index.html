The leak nobody watches

Phantom inventory is a hole in a row you can't see from the admin.

Returns portals push a restock when a return completes. None of them check that it actually happened, and most small stores have no portal at all — they rely on the Restock checkbox on the refund screen. One unchecked box, one warehouse hire who doesn't know the process, and the count drifts from the shelf. You only notice when something says "in stock" and isn't.

What the audit detects

Four ways a returned unit goes missing.

Rule A

Refunded, not restocked

A refund was issued with the Restock box unchecked. The money went back; the unit never did.

Rule B

Open return, aging

A return is in progress and the unit hasn't come back yet — tracked and aged so nothing stalls silently.

Rule C

Confirmed loss

A return was dispositioned as not-restocked or missing. A known write-off, surfaced and counted.

Growth

Rule D · Restock claimed, stock unchanged

A restock was recorded, but the on-hand level never moved. The check no portal runs on itself.

What we can, and can't, see

An honest auditor doesn't claim to see the shelf.

StockReclaim reports where the record and the adjustment disagree. It never claims a physical count, and never says an item is definitely gone — only that the paperwork doesn't reconcile.

Read-mostly by design

The scan never writes. The one and only write is Restock now — and only when you click it on a specific exception.

Nothing of yours is mirrored

No products, orders, refunds or inventory are copied or stored. The scan reads live and discards.

Why it's a subscription, not a one-off

Finding the leak is the easy part. Proving it stays closed is the job.

The person who forgets to tick Restock changes every few months, and every new warehouse hire is a new leak. A weekly digest and a running recovery ledger keep the number in front of you, so a clean quarter this quarter doesn't become a blind one next.
